## How to spend a weekend in Porto

A station worth entering without a ticket. A bridge worth crossing for the evening. A tram that takes you towards the Atlantic. This is a weekend in Porto. 🇵🇹

📍 Ribeira — cover
Colourful houses, riverside terraces and traditional boats on the Douro. Make this your lunch stop after walking down through the old town.

📍 Mercado do Bolhão — slide 2
Start Saturday with coffee and a browse through the produce stalls. The market is closed on Sundays, so save the coast for day two.

📍 São Bento station — slide 3
Jorge Colaço’s blue-and-white panels turn the entrance hall into a story of Portuguese history. Continue along Rua das Flores towards Ribeira afterwards.

📍 Luís I Bridge → Jardim do Morro — slide 4
Take the upper deck across to Gaia for Porto’s skyline in the changing evening light. The upper crossing is also used by the metro.

📍 Passeio Alegre → Foz do Douro — slide 5
Historic tram 1 links Infante with Passeio Alegre. From the garden, continue to the Atlantic promenade and Felgueiras lighthouse. Check the current tram service before setting off.

The simple split: Saturday for the market, old town and river; Sunday for the coast. Leave space between stops—Porto is hilly.

One date to save for another visit: the night of 23 June, when São João brings street celebrations and the toy-hammer greeting tradition. Check the next official programme before booking around a particular show.

## The French Riviera by train

The French Riviera is more than a summer road trip. Take the coastal trains—and in February, add a carnival and a parade made of citrus. 🍋

Use Nice as a base for separate outings. Antibes is west; Villefranche-sur-Mer and Menton are east. These are choices across several days, not a single-day itinerary.

📍 Menton — cover + slide 6
Pastel old-town buildings above the waterfront. For a different side of the town, visit in February for the annual Fête du Citron: elaborate citrus creations in the Biovès gardens and fruit-covered parade floats. Look for the Golden Fruit Parades or an evening parade.

📍 Promenade des Anglais, Nice — slide 2
Walk the seafront beside the Baie des Anges, then head into the old town. At Cours Saleya, try hot socca: a thin chickpea pancake. Chez Thérésa’s market stand serves it Tuesday–Sunday; Monday is antiques-market day.

📍 Villefranche-sur-Mer — slide 3
Follow the pastel lanes to Quai Courbet and Saint-Pierre chapel, painted inside by Jean Cocteau. Check its seasonal opening days before making the art stop your main reason to go.

📍 Château Grimaldi, Antibes — slide 4
Pair the Picasso Museum and sea-facing sculpture terrace with the old-town ramparts. The museum closes on Mondays.

📍 Nice Carnival — slide 5
February brings giant floats and flower battles, where costumed performers shower spectators with flowers. Check the organiser’s programme and tickets for the shows you want to see.

Festival dates change each year and some editions extend into early March—check the current programmes before booking. Check TER departures and last trains too. Sunny coast photos show the locations; event photos show past editions.

Save this for a car-free coast escape.

## 5 places in Japan beyond the big cities

Japan has sand dunes by the sea, a village built for deep snow and wild monkeys that choose to soak in hot springs. These five places deserve a spot on your wishlist. 🇯🇵

📍 Takachiho Gorge, Miyazaki — cover
A waterfall drops between volcanic cliffs in Kyushu. See Manai Falls from the gorge path or, when conditions allow, from a rental rowing boat. Reserve ahead and check the latest boat and trail notices.

📍 Tottori Sand Dunes — slide 2
Climb the wind-shaped dunes for views of the Sea of Japan. They're coastal dunes, not a desert; buses run from Tottori Station.

📍 Shirakawa-go, Gifu — slide 3
A lived-in UNESCO village of gassho-zukuri farmhouses with steep thatched roofs built for heavy snow. Visit by bus from Takayama; winter scenes are seasonal.

📍 Jigokudani Snow Monkey Park, Nagano — slide 4
Wild Japanese macaques sometimes bathe in the hot spring, especially in winter (roughly December–March). The approach includes a forest walk of around 25–40 minutes. Sightings and bathing aren't guaranteed; never touch or feed the monkeys.

📍 Shirogane Blue Pond, Biei, Hokkaido — slide 5
Minerals and light make this pond appear strikingly blue, but its colour changes with weather and season. Reach it by local bus or car from Biei; the blue water isn't guaranteed.

These are separate discoveries across Japan, not a five-stop route or a one-week itinerary. The cover shows a green-season gorge; Shirakawa-go and the monkeys show winter, while the pond and closing landscape show autumn.
