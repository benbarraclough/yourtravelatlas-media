# Your Travel Atlas — Editorial Playbook v6

Owner's instructions consolidated 28–29 October 2026. This document is the editorial source of truth for the @yourtravelatlas automated publication. Existing code checks are guardrails, not a substitute for editorial judgment.

## Publication standard — v6 discovery-first (supersedes v5)

Owner calibration, 29 October 2026: v5 became too much like a weekend brochure. Model hospitality.guide's simplicity, photographic impact, clear hooks and location-led captions. Create original selections and copy with properly licensed imagery. Read EDITORIAL_REVIEW.md and EDITORIAL_BENCHMARKS.md each run.

**Cover:** one striking authentic photograph and one plain, gripping title that clearly states the subject/promise. Examples: “How to spend a weekend in Porto”, “Road trips to take before you die”, “The best places to see the northern lights”. Do not add a competing destination hierarchy, subtitle, masthead or deck. A small @yourtravelatlas and issue number are sufficient. Remove WANDER FURTHER from new carousels. Published issue001 remains frozen.

**Supporting pages:** photography leads. Use an exact place/subject name and concise, specific information about what it actually is and why it matters. Maximum **30 words total editorial overlay** per supporting slide, including heading, dates and archive labels; usually less. This is a ceiling, never a target. Do not require every page to explain an itinerary or action. A wildlife post may explain the animal and where it lives; a fact post may reveal one surprising verified fact; a road trip may show distance; a rail post may name the stop and worthwhile experience. Essential context belongs on the slide if its absence would mislead. Fuller timing, access, route and booking detail belongs in the caption. No default cream planning panels, dense paragraphs or repeated generic “wander and café” advice.

**Caption:** a gripping accurate opening; 📍 exact spots matched to slides; concise place-specific explanations and the information a traveller actually wants to know. Distance for road trips, season for aurora/wildlife, route orientation for rail, significant closures or confirmed event dates when relevant. The critical information must be available within the carousel/caption, not hidden behind an external guide. Stay within 2,200 characters including credits and hashtags. No generic enthusiasm, fake secrets or guaranteed wildlife/aurora/weather.

**Evergreen by default:** avoid building ordinary posts around a particular year or upcoming edition. Use verified recurring months/seasons (“visit in February for the Lemon Festival”), with a short caption note that exact dates vary and should be checked. Do not imply a festival lasts all month or happens on identical dates forever. Exact year-specific dates stay in the research ledger unless a deliberately timely concept genuinely needs them. Archive photo context remains accurate. Recheck recurrence and material practical claims before reuse.

**Freshness:** discover cool places, experiences, facts and visual stories first, across accessible Instagram posts, TikTok/Reels, travel blogs, websites and books. Use a genuinely varied selection of sources over time, not a mandatory search of every medium on each run. Record exact sources inspected and never claim to have read/watched material that was inaccessible. Develop multiple possible ideas, then choose on hook clarity, photographic potential, curiosity, shareability, factual substance and portfolio difference. Source travel claims independently from primary evidence. Broad lists are welcome alongside focused guides; weekends must remain occasional, not the account's default.

**Slide count follows the idea:** 5–10 total currently supported, including exactly one final follow card. No fixed count and no five-editorial-page minimum. This technical range is not an editorial target: a top-ten collection cannot be promised in a pipeline that only has room for eight supporting item pages. Either design multiple clearly legible items per page, expand and test the pipeline within current platform limits, or choose an honest smaller-number hook. Never silently omit promised items or pad to the maximum.

**Closing card:** one scenic, licensed image, not repeated within the post. “Fill your feed with wonder.” / “Follow @yourtravelatlas for beautiful places and the details that turn them into real plans.” Keep it recognisable and uncluttered. No false algorithmic urgency. Reels retain their separate format.

**Approval:** source checks, per-slide value/context/detail/contribution reviews, actual full-size and mobile image inspection, caption review and a review record remain mandatory. Judge curiosity, visual appeal, clarity, specific substance and fit to the post's intention—not whether every slide contains a complete trip plan. Ben approved the exact first v6 Porto and Riviera previews/captions on 29 October 2026 and explicitly requested scheduling. Their temporary owner-review hold is released; the approved revisions and verified schedules are in content/reviews/. Subsequent posts proceed autonomously through the calibrated internal editorial gates unless Ben changes this policy.

New/unpublished briefs require editorial_version>=6. A successful render or completed metadata cannot grant editorial approval. Test omission/word-count/layout checks and inspect every final actual image.

## Mission and voice
A premium-feeling, curious, original Instagram travel magazine that sparks the desire to explore. Not a sales page, affiliate funnel, destination encyclopedia, AI-photo dump, generic listicle farm or safety leaflet. Showcase places, experiences, routes and ways of travelling, whether adventurous, relaxed, urban, cultural or remote. Speak to the reader as a curious traveller, not like a tourism brochure. New carousels use only discreet @yourtravelatlas and issue branding.

## Accessibility and energy balance — HARD EDITORIAL REQUIREMENT
The publication must welcome travellers who want *easy, sociable, achievable trips*, not just physically demanding destinations. Think a weekend in a beautiful town, a gentle coastal escape, cities and neighbourhoods, good food, beaches, comfortable holidays, art, culture, easy train trips, scenic drives, spontaneous breaks and unusual low-effort discoveries as well as hardcore adventures.

Target a flexible long-run mix: approximately **40% easy exploration, 35% journeys and approachable experiences, 25% demanding adventure**. This is a portfolio balance, NOT a rigid per-post classifier or a license to force ideas into a formula. Some destinations suit a mix of easy days and more demanding options; show that range. Do not assume everyone wants to hike a mountain, surf an expert wave, spend a fortnight driving remote roads or pay for expensive excursions.

**Initial corrective action completed:** unpublished issues 002 and 003 were replaced with attainable city/culture and coastal-rail ideas. Continue the long-run energy mix rather than repeatedly producing European city breaks. Avoid three consecutive expedition-style concepts. Suggestions should spark ideas the account owner might want to pursue too, not require heroics or deep pockets.

For every future issue, record **effort level** (`relaxed` / `moderate` / `adventurous`) plus an **accessibility angle** in the JSON. Explain how an ordinary traveller could enjoy it with a friend, without presenting accessible places as boring. Do not fabricate budgets or public transport access.

## Music and audio policy
- **Static image carousels:** PUBLISH WITHOUT MUSIC. Metricool/Instagram's official API cannot auto-attach music to image carousels. Never switch to manual posting just to add sound, since no routine admin is the user's goal.
- **Future authentic video Reels:** Select ambient, warm, atmospheric, understated instrumental or location-appropriate natural sounds only, consistent with the place and pace. Prefer real ambient sound when it adds meaning. No obnoxious or irrelevant viral songs, aggressive drops, comedy noises, forced pop soundtracks, or unlicensed re-uploaded "original" music.
- Only use properly cleared tracks for the Instagram account and exact usage, preferably Meta Sound Collection or demonstrably owned original sound. Never infer that "trending" means licensed.
- Metricool can add Reel audio when the connection uses Facebook Login and supported authorised audio; if unverified or unsuitable, use natural original audio or silence. No automated Reel is allowed until the rights, audio, rendered 9:16 video and auto-publish pipeline are demonstrably tested.
- Do not ask the owner to vet ordinary music choices; skip audio when confidence is not high.

## Every story begins with a discovery

Research distinctive places, routes, animals, cultural events, film locations, outdoor activities and surprising facts before selecting a concept. Build a small candidate slate; judge hook clarity, real visual potential, curiosity, save/share reason and difference from recent issues. Then commission the strongest idea and source its exact images. Reject weak material rather than forcing a concept around whatever free photos exist.

Record intent, reader promise, scope, effort/accessibility where relevant, original angle and share reason privately. A collection can simply inspire a wishlist; it need not become a timed itinerary. A factual discovery must still be compelling for travel. Do not manufacture action verbs or multi-step logistics to satisfy a form.

Mix continents, scales and intentions: occasional weekend guides; broad destinations; road trips with distances; aurora; animals; rail; national parks; fairytale villages; destination facts; film locations; outdoor activities. These are starting points, not a rotation to repeat mechanically. Do not publish consecutive near-identical formats or choose another European city/coast break just because imagery is available. Broader inspiration and easy trips are both welcome.

## Cover, supporting slides and caption

Follow the v6 standard at the top of this file. Cover = photograph + one clear hook + discreet account/issue. Supporting slides = exact subject plus specific description, at most 30 words including context labels. Keep the subject visible and type readable on a phone. Do not copy a competitor's exact layout, text or assets.

Adapt information to intention: weekend stops, road distance, aurora season, named animal/habitat, railway stop, park character, village identity, verified fact, precise film scene/location, or activity. If the photograph and the concise description fail to make the idea interesting, reject that page. Do not fix weak imagery with more words.

Caption = strong opening + slide-matched 📍 locations + relevant details and critical caveats. Draft two hooks, select the strongest accurate one. Put useful material before credits/hashtags and remain within 2,200 characters. A caption pin is a named location marker, not a claim that an Instagram geotag or interactive map was added.

## Editorial diversity / trend sensitivity
Alternate continents, environments, budget styles, trip lengths, traveller types, season and formats. Mix city deep-dives, hiking, unusual experiences, cultural stories, rail, surfing, scenic road trips, skiing, hidden corners and broader picture-book inspiration.
Occasionally draw on genuine contemporary travel conversations and seasonally timely trip-planning interests, only when supported by current, credible sources. Never chase silly viral gimmicks.
Consider Instagram Reels when there is verified, properly licensed and accurately located video, lawful audio, a designed hook, useful story, and an *actually tested free* rendering-to-publishing route. A static carousel is not a Reel.

## Research and provenance gates (must pass before scheduling)
- Verify all factual assertions against reputable current sources, preferably official tourism bodies, transport operators, park agencies, official destination information and primary evidence. Make no unverifiable price, route duration, legal access, seasonality, permit or opening-hour claims.
- Validate **each actual photo**: real destination and activity, visually appropriate to its exact label, accurate source page, author and explicit reusable licence (no Unsplash+), suitable 4:5 crop, resolution and independent impact. A declared licence string or successful JPEG download alone does **not** prove these things.
- No realistic AI-generated fake scenery passed off as authentic. No watermarks, brands, promotions or affiliation without specific authorisation.
- Check the full content archive for previously used location angles, photographic subjects, photo IDs, misleadingly similar photos, concept duplication and issue-number collisions.
- Validate the entire rendered output visually: especially cover headline hierarchy, text legibility, spelling, photography, crop, on-screen relevance, no warning captions. Correct and regenerate if it falls short.
- Prefer not publishing to publishing inaccurate, inappropriate, repetitive or low-quality material.

## Reels — cinematic discovery with a mandatory inspirational quality gate

**Footage-first source research:** Read [FOOTAGE_SOURCES.md](FOOTAGE_SOURCES.md) before every new Reel. Search beyond Pexels, favour genuine vertical/4K moving footage, verify exact asset rights/location and portrait framing, and test actual download/render delivery. Stock catalogue size or a successful sample download is not creative approval. Use one strong experience, journey, discovery, atmosphere or occasional collection as the intention; do not force another city montage. If five genuinely strong purposeful moving shots cannot be found, reject/rework or skip the Reel. This rule preserves the existing publishing cadence and all music/review requirements.
- **Independent series:** Reels have their own internal `reel_number` sequence separate from carousel issue numbering. Do not show a number or clip counter publicly.
- **Layout:** very small YOUR TRAVEL ATLAS at the top, legible destination at the top, and a concise **inspiring editorial hook or plausible experience** near the lower portion (but above Instagram interface controls), with elegant contrasting serif. **Do not put WANDER FURTHER on the Reel at all**; new photo carousels also omit the tagline. Avoid overlaying generic lists of the available footage. Evaluate mobile contrast and keep graphic elements minimal.
- **Editorial test — hard gate:** Ask whether the Reel makes a viewer want to visit the place or send a concrete trip idea to their friends, *even without reading the caption*. The video needs a compelling beginning, independently striking moving shots and a sense of progression or experience; do not just assemble whatever free footage exists. Five genuinely different, purposeful shots minimum (normally 5–8). If the video feels routine, generic or underwhelming, **reject the entire concept** and choose another. A completed MP4 is not evidence of inspiration.
- **Music — professional and upbeat:** Use warm, positive, modern, properly recorded indie-pop, lively but tasteful instrumental, soft house, acoustic and naturally rhythmic travel music that supports the images. No sombre pads, generic MIDI-sounding synthesized backing, obnoxious or unrelated meme songs. The mood and emotional match are editorial quality gates, not satisfied by an AAC track alone. Research music choices from successful travel Reels without copying or assuming popular audio is cleared.

## Technical process and zero-extra-cost constraint
1. Inspect the connected Metricool account/queued posts and the GitHub repo content archive. Review the recent portfolio for diversity of travel energy, geography, accessibility and experience type, and consult reference publications for lessons on original ideas, shareability and usefulness.
2. Select a differentiated premise, define travel effort and an easy/approachable alternative when applicable, a friend-worthy share hook and plausible experiences, research facts, sources, photos and traveller questions before choosing design. Reject underwhelming concepts before production even when factually safe.
3. Storyboard every planned slide: clear hook, exact subject, concise specific description (30 words maximum) and source references. Reconsider the entire post when the story is not inspiring, differentiated and shareable.
4. Prepare the issue JSON in \`content/posts/\` following existing examples, using unique issue number and distinct 5–10 high-resolution licensed photos.
5. GitHub Actions renders actual JPEGs through \`scripts/render.py\` into \`media/<slug>/\` and writes \`manifest.json\`. Diagnose failures; never publish from a manifest alone.
6. Inspect all actual images, check that facts and licences remain credible, then schedule through existing Metricool draft UUID or create next slot only after the applicable internal/owner approval gate. Use final public raw GitHub image URLs; media-alt-text matches images, \`draft: false\`, \`autoPublish: true\`.
7. Re-fetch Metricool queue, confirm posted dates, new media count, full caption, alt text, pending status, no duplicate; never claim post is live before confirmed.
8. Measure saves/shares/follows/reach and Reels watch time once available. Test Monday evenings, Wednesday noon, Thursday mornings as starting UK slots, optimise later based on real account metrics. Maximum 20 posts/month on free plan.
9. Maintain existing recurring ChatGPT automation; fail closed if a required free tool, reliable licence, accuracy check or rendering step is unavailable. Zero additional spending beyond the existing ChatGPT subscription.
