## Creative freedom and scroll-stopping hooks
- Before selecting visuals, write a specific, compelling **cover premise**: an intriguing experience, surprising fact, unusual collection, strong verified superlative, practical ambition or a vivid contrast. The first slide must offer a distinctive reason to swipe; a place name with '5 amazing spots' alone is rarely enough. Examples of the *kind* of angle to explore (NOT pre-approved factual titles or mandatory ideas): 'Places where you can walk through a jungle', 'Snowboarding destinations worth travelling for', 'Villages so remote they feel like another world', 'Rail journeys with extraordinary views', 'Swim spots hidden in dramatic landscapes'. Balance aspirational discovery with concrete value.
- For thematic and multi-country carousels, make a proper coherent cross-location selection and ensure the cover's geographic kicker is truthful, e.g. WORLDWIDE, EUROPE, THE ALPS, or a country name only if every place is in it. Do NOT force a country label onto a worldwide collection. Thematic posts must have specific named places and a reason each is worth visiting, not generic imagery and superficial adjectives.
- Do not fabricate 'world's best', 'most isolated', '10', or other numerical/ranking claims. Research the ranking criterion if making a factual superlative; otherwise frame an editorial selection without pretending an objective list exists. If the hook promises 10, deliver ten actual places/experiences (up to 10 Instagram photo slides; a cover with multiple named examples or other editorial solutions if needed), or revise the hook to fit the approved media.
- Give people a memorable travel idea they'd want to save or share. Mix specific destination discoveries, beautiful regional experiences, unexpected thematic lists, culture, beaches, scenic rail journeys, food, cities and accessible holidays with occasional adventures. Choose the strongest original concepts without rigid quotas, mandatory comparison of three ideas, forced continents, or compulsory trends.
- Each carousel needs **at least five relevant photos** with a genuinely jaw-dropping, visually honest cover, a gripping clear place-and-premise hook, and enough distinct real sights/experiences to paint a coherent, useful story. A technically good but ordinary image is NOT enough; reject dull/flat/weak supporting images and seek better originals. Place a small truthful geographic kicker immediately ABOVE the title on every new cover (brief field `cover_country`: country for one-country features; WORLDWIDE, region/continent for cross-border topics). Use up to Instagram's supported 10 slides if needed; one scenic follow/brand ending only when it adds to the story, not by reflex. If a title promises a count, show the full count.
- Every **slideshow Reel** needs 5–10 distinct properly licensed still photographs, each visually compelling and clearly relevant; make a purpose-built 9:16 short video with tasteful Ken Burns motion, concise location-specific text, strong opening hook and coherent flow. Use `scripts/render_photo_reel.py` via the existing `photo_slideshow_v1` support inside `scripts/render_reel.py`. The existing code was previously archived as an experiment but is now an explicitly approved weekly publication format. Do not revive the old rejected Amalfi slideshow; make fresh briefs with rights evidence. No music/audio.
- Every **genuine-footage Reel** needs at least five distinct real moving-footage clips, a clear narrative, strong video transitions and meaningful descriptive on-screen text. Only schedule genuine footage when clips are actually available, correctly licensed, technically supported and reviewed end-to-end. Otherwise substitute a second slideshow Reel for that slot. Do not label still images as genuine moving footage. No music/audio.
- Strong cover, intentionally beautiful photography, easily readable small brand, short varied editorial captions/on-screen labels. Keep location and the important specific reward clear. Captions: fresh short hook, useful 📍 places, one standout reason/experience each, compact credits and optional invitation to save/share. **No travel-advisory essay, pointless caveats, multiple seasons, unnecessary disclaimers or logistics.** State only material restrictions when omission would materially mislead.
- No public issue numbers, no Wander Further, no promotions, no paid tools or new subscriptions; creative decisions and normal internal approval fully delegated.

## Instagram formats and production sizes — do not confuse them
- **Swipeable feed carousel = Instagram POST**, normally 5–10 separate portrait JPGs **1080×1350, 4:5** in this repository. The viewer manually swipes image-by-image and can pause on every location; ensure a consistent aspect ratio for all pages. The production renderer is `scripts/render.py` and `instagramData.type=POST`. Instagram's native app now supports some 3:4 photos but 4:5 remains this project's tested reliable format via Metricool/API. Do NOT export these pages as a single video.
- **Slideshow Reel = Instagram REEL**, a single automatically playing **1080×1920, 9:16** silent MP4 constructed from 5–10 distinct licensed still photos, with readable overlays and subtle movement/transitions. The viewer cannot manually swipe between its images. **This is now a deliberate and required weekly content type** (one per week, or two when real video cannot be made). Source/render: `content/reels/<slug>.json` with `treatment=photo_slideshow_v1`, processed by `scripts/render_reel.py` and its `scripts/render_photo_reel.py` helper. Do not misrepresent the stills as actual video footage.
- **Genuine video Reel = Instagram REEL**, **1080×1920, 9:16**, encoded as one MP4 from **at least five distinct real moving-footage clips**. The reel advances automatically; use `scripts/render_reel.py` with a genuine-motion brief and `instagramData.type=REEL`. Keep important overlay text safe from Instagram UI and profile-grid crop. No music/audio. Test the moving finished video, not just source stills. If not feasible, use the slideshow Reel format instead.
- Check final image/video dimensions, codecs, type, crop/cover, publication method and auto-publishing status against Metricool before booking. **4:5 POST and 9:16 REEL are deliberately different outputs.**

## Genuine visuals & rights — essential
Choose concepts partly for the availability of truly spectacular *sourceable* visuals. Search **Pexels** (photos & video, also accessible as an image bank in Metricool), original free **Unsplash** (exclude Unsplash+), and exact-file **Wikimedia Commons** (CC0/public domain or properly satisfied CC licence). For motion, search Pexels first, then evaluate **Pixabay**, **Mixkit Free** (never Restricted), original non-AI/non-sponsored **Coverr** footage, suitable **Dareful** CC BY clips, exact-file **Wikimedia Commons**, and individually verified public-domain **US National Park Service** media. For photographs also consider licensed Pixabay, exact Commons/Flickr Creative Commons sources, and permitted public-archive originals. The 29 October source audit in `docs/reference/FOOTAGE_SOURCES.md` downloaded/decoded sample MP4s from Pixabay, Mixkit and Coverr, but this **does not mean those providers are all integrated into the live Reel renderer**. Existing `scripts/render_reel.py` accepts limited media hosts/licences; check current code and test each new source's allowed download, licence, location accuracy, crop, video decode, and GitHub Actions render before using it. Never quietly fall back to poor-quality, low-resolution or AI-made clips just to produce a Reel. Never rip other Instagram creators' footage, tourism-board video without licensed permission, or auto-generate fake destination scenes. A photographer credit alone does not grant reuse rights. Verify on the actual asset page the origin, creator, licence allowing publication and editing, exact place/identity and full-size quality; avoid brand/person releases or unverified location claims. Prefer original portrait/4K quality to poor 9:16 crops. Keep per-photo page URLs/licence evidence compactly in the media brief; credit appropriately in captions. Do not use a provider just because the library as a whole is described as free.

## Quick final check
For each complete final post/reel ask: (1) Would this particular opening make a new viewer curious enough to swipe, and is the specific experience/promise clear? (2) Real accurate strong images or actual clips, each relevant? (3) An interesting story someone would save/share? (4) Text/cover mobile legible, no ugly crop? (5) Caption light, accurate, correct photo credits? (6) Exact sources and commercial reuse permission checked? (7) Correct file format and no audio in a silent Reel? If any answer is no, make a focused fix, re-render and check again; if the concept fundamentally fails, change concept instead. Do not require a seven-gate report, multi-stage handoff, or extensive status paperwork; a single brief and concise final summary suffice.
